Writing Good Additional Context

Additional Context is the box on the launch screen (the "Add context" tile)
where you tell the classifier something only a person knows — a property sold
mid-quarter, a counterparty whose name always means one of our deals, a vendor
that is always routine for us. This page explains why it is the most powerful
input you have, and how to phrase it so the model actually obeys it. If you'd
rather not hand-write it, download the skill at the bottom and let Claude build
it for you.


WHY THIS BOX OUTRANKS EVERYTHING
------------------------------------------------------------

The classifier reads its inputs in tiers. The rulebook (the doctrine) is the
default, the known-deal index is a lookup aid, and this box — your context — is
the top authority. When you write an explicit rule here and its conditions are
met by a row or its invoice, the model applies the outcome you specified,
literally, ahead of its own standard judgment. It will also name your rule in
its reasoning whenever that rule is in play, so you can audit that each one did
what you meant.

That power only works if the context is phrased as a rule the model can act on.
"Watch out for the Redwood stuff" gives it nothing to fire on. "Any charge whose
invoice names 1600 Market St dated on or after 2025-03-31 is non-recurring"
gives it an exact condition and an exact outcome. Same knowledge, phrased to be
obeyed.


THE ONE THING THAT MAKES A RULE FIRE
------------------------------------------------------------

A condition can only be checked against what is actually written on a row or in
its attached invoice — a name, a date, a keyword, an account number. It cannot
see what you know in your head. So if the tie is pure insider knowledge, pin the
rule to the handle that does show up on those rows: the vendor or payee, the
matter number, the entity id, the invoice number. When in doubt, ask yourself
"what actually appears on those rows that we can key off of?" and write the rule
around that.


WHAT GOOD CONTEXT LOOKS LIKE
------------------------------------------------------------

The strongest context is a short list of specific rules, each with three parts:
what to look for (a name, keyword, or date test), where to look (the row, its
invoice, or both), and what outcome to apply (non-recurring, human review, or —
if you're sure it isn't routine but don't want to pick — just "flag" and let the
doctrine choose). Give each rule its own plain-language intent so the reasoning
can cite it back to you.

Keep it tight — a handful of precise rules beats a page of prose every time. And
remember: typing into the box is a one-time, this-run-only override. A note you
want to keep should be saved into the deal-context file so it carries to the
next quarter.


THE STRUCTURED (XML) FORMAT
------------------------------------------------------------

Rules written as a small XML block — one condition-to-outcome mandate each —
are the most legible to the model and the most reliably obeyed. You do not have
to author that by hand. Download the skill below, load it into your own Claude,
paste in whatever notes you already have (or none), and it will interview you
for the missing specifics and hand back a ready-to-paste block. Copy that block
into the Additional Context box, and the classifier treats it as top-tier
mandate on the next run.


USING THE SKILL IN YOUR OWN CLAUDE
------------------------------------------------------------

Once you've downloaded SKILL.md, open a chat in your own Claude and drag the
file straight into the message box, then send it. Claude reads it and steps into
the deal-context-builder role. From there, just talk to it: tell it what you
know in plain words, and paste in whatever you already have in the context box.
It will ask a few follow-up questions to pin down the specifics, then hand back
a finished block for you to copy into Additional Context.


HOW TO ABSTRACT WHAT YOU KNOW SO THE AI READS IT WELL
------------------------------------------------------------

The way you phrase something matters as much as what you say. The model reads a
specific, structured rule far better than a paragraph of narrative, so aim for
the same shape the skill writes out. Give each fact its own rule, and give that
rule three parts: a trigger (what to look for), a place to look (the row, its
invoice, or both), and a result (non-recurring, human review, or just flag it).

Two habits do most of the work. First, one fact per rule -- don't fold three
situations into one sentence; the model applies clean, separate rules far more
reliably than a compound one. Second, tie every rule to something that actually
appears on the row or its invoice -- a vendor name, a matter number, an entity,
an invoice number, a date -- never to knowledge that lives only in your head. If
the only thing you truly know is "row 1196 is the Bush deal," find what shows up
on that row that the model can see (the payee, the matter number) and key the
rule to that instead.

Then trim. Drop the hedging and the backstory and keep the decision. "This
vendor sometimes does deal work but usually doesn't, so maybe keep an eye on it"
gives the model nothing; "flag any charge from this vendor whose invoice names a
matter number" gives it an exact trigger and an exact result. The tighter and
more concrete you are, the more reliably the classifier does what you meant --
and the skill is built to push you toward exactly that.
