What happens when you run this

You give it your two source workbooks. They get combined into one list of
rows to work through.

Before anything else, rows that represent a company acquisition or sale are
pulled out and looked at first. These build a reference list of known deals
that gets used later, and this list carries over between runs instead of
starting over each time.

Every other row is then given a decision: recurring cost, non-recurring
cost, or needs a person to review it. The reference list of known deals,
plus any extra notes you've provided, is used to inform every decision.
Rows that have a usable invoice are looked at one at a time, including the
invoice's contents. Rows without a usable invoice are grouped together and
processed in batches, since there's no invoice content to worry about
mixing up.

The result is one Excel file containing:
- Every original row, with the decision, the reasoning behind it,
  supporting evidence, and the invoice's read status added on.
- A separate sheet listing only the rows worth a person's attention
  (anything flagged as non-recurring or needing review), grouped by vendor,
  with totals. Acquisition/sale rows are left out of this sheet since those
  are decided by fixed rule, not judgment.
- A separate sheet showing the reference list of known deals that was
  actually used to make decisions, so you can see what the reasoning was
  based on.
- A summary sheet with overall counts and cost.
