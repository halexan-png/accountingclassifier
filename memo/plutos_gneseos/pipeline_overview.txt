WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU RUN THIS

You supply two source workbooks. They're combined into one worklist.

Before anything else, the rows describing a company acquisition or sale
are pulled out and read first. They build a reference list of known
deals — the vocabulary the rest of the run will recognize — and that list
carries forward between runs instead of being rebuilt from nothing each
time.

Every other row is then given a verdict: recurring cost, non-recurring
cost, or a case for a person to review. The known-deal list, together
with whatever additional notes you've supplied, informs each of those
verdicts. Rows with a usable invoice are read one at a time, invoice
included. Rows without one are grouped and processed in batches, since
there's no invoice content at risk of bleeding from one row into another.

What comes out is a single Excel file, containing:

- Every original row, with the verdict, the reasoning behind it, the
  supporting evidence, and the invoice's read status appended.
- A separate sheet listing only the rows that deserve a person's
  attention — anything flagged non-recurring or for human review —
  grouped by vendor, with totals. Acquisition and sale rows are left off
  this sheet; those are settled by fixed rule, not judgment.
- A separate sheet showing the known-deal list actually used to make
  these decisions, so the reasoning behind them can be checked.
- A summary sheet with overall counts and cost.
