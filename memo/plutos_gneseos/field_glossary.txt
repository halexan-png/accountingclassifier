FIELD GLOSSARY: COLUMNS, FLAGS, AND DEAL-PROFILE FIELDS

The exact meaning of every column and flag the pipeline adds to your
data, and every field in the deal profile sheet — reference material for
looking something up mid-review. For the operational behaviors these
fields reflect (a stopped run, a truncated invoice, a rough deal sweep),
see Specifics -> Risk & Reference Notes.


DOWNLOADED FILE COLUMNS
------------------------------------------------------------

Fourteen columns are added to your original data:

CLASSIFICATION — the verdict: recurring, non-recurring, human review,
skipped (negative amount), or reclass.

BASIS — what drove the verdict: a system-close rule, an invoice's own
contents, a recognized deal, the row's routine-sounding text on its own,
a reclass keyword, a fixed M&A-account rule, or nothing identifiable.

PHASE — which stage produced the decision: the opening M&A sweep, or the
main classification pass.

HAD_INVOICE — whether the row pointed to an invoice at all, by link or
by a number found in its own text.

INVOICE_ACCESSED — whether a document was actually opened and read,
independent of what it said once opened.

INVOICE_READ — the model's own claim about the invoice it was given,
kept separate from INVOICE_ACCESSED so the two can be checked against
each other.

INVOICE_DATE — whatever date the model found on the invoice, when there
was one to find.

REASONING — the model's own account of why it landed on this verdict.

EVIDENCE — the specific text or invoice detail it's pointing to in
support of that reasoning.

MISSING_INFO — human-review rows only: the exact open question that
kept the row from being settled outright.

INVOICE_POINTER — where the invoice actually came from: the link it
followed, or the local file and the page range it read.

INVOICE_ERROR — explains why, when an invoice was referenced but never
read, it couldn't be.

FLAGS — whatever notable conditions attach to the row; see Flag Values,
below.

DEAL_SWEEP_STATUS — M&A-account rows only: what happened when the
opening sweep tried to gather this row's deal information.


FLAG VALUES
------------------------------------------------------------

Thirteen flags can appear in the FLAGS column, alone or in combination:

CLOSEGL_USER — a system-close entry, automatically recurring, never
sent to the model at all.

RECLASS_RULE — the row's own text contained the word "reclass," so it
was labeled a bookkeeping reclassification in the very first pass,
before the model ever saw it.

SKIPPED_NEGATIVE — the row carried a negative dollar amount and was set
aside for this run.

HAD_INVOICE — the row pointed to an invoice, whether by link or by a
number found in its own text.

INVOICE_ACCESSED — a document was actually opened and read.

INVOICE_UNAVAILABLE — an invoice was referenced but could not be read.

DEAL_PROFILE_MATCH — the row's text matched something in the known-deal
list. A suspicion only; the doctrine still has to confirm it before it
becomes a verdict.

AMOUNT_BLANK — the dollar amount was blank, or a dead formula.

DEAL_SWEEP_FAILED — the opening sweep tried and failed to gather this
row's deal information.

DEAL_SWEEP_SKIPPED — this row wasn't swept at all, either because it
fell outside the quarters selected or because the sweep relied on a
reused deal list instead.

BASIS_MISMATCH — the model claimed it was relying on a known deal, but
hadn't actually recognized one. Rather than guess at the real reason,
the record is quietly corrected to show no identifiable basis.

INVOICE_READ_MISMATCH — an invoice was sent to the model, but what it
reports about reading it doesn't line up with what actually happened.
Flagged for whoever's auditing the row; not corrected automatically.

INVOICE_TRUNCATED — the invoice ran long enough that only its opening
and closing pages were read (see Specifics -> Risk & Reference Notes,
item 3). Whatever sat in the middle may have gone unseen.


DEAL PROFILE SHEET COLUMNS
------------------------------------------------------------

Ten columns describe each known deal:

NAME — the deal's settled canonical name. SUPPORTING_ROWS — which rows
in this run actually contributed to the entry. MATTER_NUMBERS and
INVOICE_NUMBERS — the legal matter and invoice numbers seen tied to it.
QUARTERS — which quarters' activity have touched it so far. ALIASES —
other names or spellings the system has learned mean the same deal.
PROPERTIES and ENTITYIDS — the properties and entity IDs named in
connection with it. ADVISORS_SEEN — the advisors or vendors associated
with it. EVIDENCE — the specific text supporting why the entry exists at
all.


HOW DEALS ARE MATCHED AND MERGED
------------------------------------------------------------

Deals are matched by name, without regard to capitalization — the same
name seen twice, however it's cased, is treated as one deal, not two.
When a match is found, nothing is thrown away and nothing is duplicated:
every list on the entry — aliases, matter numbers, invoice numbers,
properties, entity IDs, advisors, supporting rows, the quarters touched
— is combined with what's already there, and only genuinely new evidence
is appended to what's already recorded.

The practical effect is that a deal doesn't fragment or dilute just
because it resurfaces under a slightly different spelling next quarter.
It accumulates instead — one entry, growing richer each time the deal
reappears, rather than multiplying.
